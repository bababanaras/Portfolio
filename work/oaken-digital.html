Fairstone Bank · 2023–25 · Digital banking

Oaken Digital: banking customers over 60 can finish on their own

I led the redesign of Oaken's digital banking platform, turning a costly, assisted model into a digital-first, zero-touch experience for a customer base where nearly 80% are over 60.

Role
Senior Manager, Product Design, leading a team of 5 designers
Scope
Onboarding, authenticated banking and originations

The problem

Built for confident users. Used by customers over 60.

The platform was optimized for digitally confident users, but nearly 80% of active customers were over 60. The result was confusion, high abandonment and very few straight-through account openings: only 20% were zero-touch.

Left alone, account opening would keep leaning on call centres and branches, older customers would lose trust in digital channels, and Oaken would never scale as a digital-first bank.

Research

What digital-first banks were getting right

  • Mobile-first, task-focused journeys with few decision points
  • Plain language, progressive disclosure and strong visual hierarchy
  • Success measured by completion rates, not feature depth

Competitors optimized for confidence, not intelligence. That was our gap.

Discovery

Why customers were failing, not just where

Funnel tracking told us where people dropped off. To learn why, we went a level deeper: drop-off at every onboarding step, time on task, and form-level abandonment and error rates. Identity verification, document upload and long-form entry were the hardest moments.

We spent a year building Power BI dashboards that joined API events, telemetry and app analytics into one trusted view. The app data was stark: 70% of applications started in the app were never finished, and only 3% of all applications were submitted there.

The core issue was clarity and flow, not a lack of intent.

Strategy

Four principles to design for zero assistance

  • Clarity over cleverness. Plain language and obvious next steps beat novel patterns.
  • One primary action per screen. Every screen asks for one decision, and only one.
  • Progressive disclosure. Complexity appears only when the user needs it.
  • Design for zero assistance. If someone would need to call us, the screen has failed.

The app

Underneath it all, an accessibility-first design system in Figma: scalable type, contrast-safe tokens, larger targets and reusable onboarding and origination components, mapped directly to code.

Validation

  • Usability testing with older users for comprehension and confidence
  • A/B tests on call-to-action language, layout density and step order
  • Engineers in discovery, annotated specs and sprint design reviews, so what we designed is what shipped

My role

I owned the end-to-end experience from strategy to outcomes, and led and mentored the UX team around one vision of clarity and accessibility. I partnered with Product, Engineering, Compliance, Legal, Analytics and Operations from day one, and used behavioural data and experiments to settle disagreements.

What I learned

  • Designing for the most vulnerable user makes the experience better for everyone.
  • Analytics tells you where; users tell you why.
  • Great banking UX is less about innovation and more about removing doubt.

Outcome

Zero-touch account openings rose from 20% to 83% across channels, with 68% engagement across a 65K-user base and 80%+ positive user feedback from 2022 to 2025. Digitizing retail enrolment cut paper-based enrolments by 50%, contact-centre call volumes fell, and the design system became the base for the next products, including the Deposit Broker Portal.

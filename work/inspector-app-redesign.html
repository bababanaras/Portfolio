TL;DR

The redesign turns the Inspector App from a form staff abandon for paper into one that finishes a move-in or move-out in one visit, with or without signal.

  • Find today's work: the default Open view cuts the list from 47 rows to 5 in the prototype's sample property (−89%), sorted by due date.
  • Fewer interactions: a full move-in drops from an est. 132 taps and scrolls to 94 on phone (−29%) and 86 on iPad (−35%).
  • Photos: documenting an item with 3 photos drops from an est. 11 taps to 5 (−55%) with burst capture.
  • Offline: every tap saves to the device first. Re-keying from paper, an est. 12–17 min per inspection on weak-signal sites, goes to zero.
  • Submission: the "Inspection already submitted" dead end is gone. Every blocker is named, grouped by room and one tap away.

Estimates come from a step-by-step model of the redesigned flows (see Workflow impact). Production KPIs, each baselined before release, are in Metrics and KPIs.

Context and problem

The app could not produce its core output: a completed, trusted inspection record. Staff fell back to paper on several active accounts.

Who uses it. Site staff, leasing agents and property managers run move-in, move-out, annual and building inspections. They walk units one-handed, often in townhomes, mobile homes and basements with no shared Wi-Fi.

Approach

Five rules drove every screen decision:

  1. Work comes to the inspector. The list opens on what is open, sorted by due date. History stays in History.
  2. The device is the record until the server confirms. Every rating, comment, signature and photo saves locally on the tap.
  3. Never block without a path. If something stops submit, the app names it, groups it by room and links to it.
  4. Ask only what applies. Templates follow inspection type. Rating scales follow item type. N/A is on every mandatory item.
  5. One status vocabulary. Six states, one pill, one header strip, used on the list, the inspection and Uploads.

How it was built. The team ran a solo, AI-assisted loop with no research team: screenshot audit and customer tickets, then a Jira epic with eight stories, then Claude Design prompts per surface. The output is a clickable prototype with a network switch (Offline, Weak, Online) that shows sync behaviour live. Field validation with Metcap and FSR is next.

Redesigned screens

Each redesigned screen answers one legacy failure. Renders come from the clickable prototype, set to Offline, which is the hardest case.

Finding work

The legacy list opened on the archive. The redesign opens on the worklist: Open by default, with a count on every scope chip so staff see the size of the day before they scroll. The offline banner names the last sync time, so a stale list never reads as a frozen app.

Inspection progress

The legacy inspection opened on unlabelled fields and a waste-vendor dropdown. The redesign opens on rooms, each with a progress bar and an "N missing" flag. The strip under the header says what is saved and what is queued.

Rating an item

Three scales in a row made staff tap by position. Now one control has three variants by item type. N/A sits apart in a dashed, neutral box. Mark Section N/A clears an absent room in one tap, and Poor or Missing asks for a photo.

Submit, deposit and sync

These surfaces had no legacy equivalent; the work happened on paper or not at all. Submit opens a review sheet listing what is left by room, with Go on each row. The deposit is a ledger read from the tenant record. Uploads lists unsynced work worst first, logout names what would be lost, and a submitted inspection opens read-only.

iPad

Rooms on the left, items on the right, comments as a third column. The comment sheet narrows the items pane instead of covering the item it annotates.

Status system

Six states replace one orange (!). The key move was separating lifecycle (where the record is) from urgency (whether the inspector is late).

Decisions

  • One lifecycle: Draft → Queued to submit → Syncing → Submitted. Retry moves Sync failed back to Syncing.
  • Overdue is a flag, not a state. It rides only on Draft, as a compound pill: "Overdue 3 days | Draft".
  • The due clock stops on the device. Finished on time but synced late carries no flag. Weak signal never makes anyone late.
  • Red means act now. Solid red is reserved for Sync failed, the only state with an action. Overdue uses tinted red.
  • Never colour alone. Every pill carries colour, icon and label: 32pt regular, 26pt compact for list rows.
  • The header strip reports save and sync only. Overdue lives in the subtitle, so the two never fight for one line. The whole strip is a 56pt tap target.
  • Type colours are reserved. Unit orange, maintenance purple and building navy never mean status.

What the review caught against the first pass (board 1a)

  • List pills had no icons.
  • "Overdue" appeared twice in one row, as a pill and as "Overdue 12 days".
  • Not Started and In Progress collapsed into Draft; progress moved to "4 of 11 rated".
  • A green Submit button in the header read as Submitted. It became a navy button.
  • Filter chips (34pt), type filter (32pt) and rating buttons (46pt) sat under 48pt. Flagged for the next pass.

Design system pieces used

  • Colour: teal #0e9090 for focus, active tabs and links. Navy #0d2b4e for primary buttons and the offline banner. Status green #1a7e3e, amber #e08c00 and red #c62828 on 10–14% tints, matching PVBadge.
  • Type: Inter only. 13/500 pill labels, 11/500 uppercase eyebrows ("SURFACES"), Title Case buttons ("Submit Inspection", "Stay and Sync").
  • Shape: 14px cards, 6px controls, hairline borders, shadow-xs on cards, the teal focus ring.
  • New field density tier: PV DS was sized for desktop admin. The app adds 48pt minimum targets and 52pt primary buttons.
  • Navy, not teal, for primary buttons: white on teal measures 3.88:1, under the WCAG AA 4.5:1 minimum.

Gaps to close before handoff (each is a likely QA bug)

  • ☐ Token hex values are hardcoded, not referenced (#0e9090 appears 29 times in the prototype, 216 in the fixes board). Swap to var(--pv-*) or dark mode and theme changes break.
  • ☐ Maintenance purple has no DS token. Add one or reassign.
  • ☐ Amber text: DS specifies #b87200; the app uses #8a5600 on tint for contrast. Pick one.
  • ☐ Promote three components used on 3+ screens: status pill (PVBadge variant), header strip (PVAlert variant), rating control (new).

Workflow impact

A full move-in takes an est. 29% fewer taps and scrolls on phone and 35% fewer on iPad. The bigger time win is what disappears: paper, re-keying and blocked submits.

Task Before After Change
Find today's inspection (47 records) Scan 47 rows: est. 5 scrolls + 1 tap Scan 5 open rows: 1 tap −83% interactions
Document one item with 3 photos est. 11 taps across two screens and the OS camera 5 taps, camera stays open −55%
Mark absent in-suite laundry N/A (3 items) Not possible; blocks submit 1 tap, Mark Section N/A Unblocked
Recover a blocked submit Dead end: "already submitted" Review sheet: Go, rate, back, 2 taps per item Path exists
Move-out deposit refund Calculated off-app on paper In-app ledger, reviewed in 1 tap Paper step removed

Room navigation (16) does not change on phone. On iPad the two-pane layout halves it, taking the total to 86.

Model assumptions (est.)

  • Full move-in template from the prototype: 41 items across 8 rooms.
  • 6 items photographed at 2 photos each; 4 canned comments; one 3-item section marked N/A.
  • Legacy photo flow per shot: Take Photo, shutter, OS confirm; plus one tap in and one back.
  • Legacy find: target sits halfway down a 47-row list at about 5 rows per screen.
  • Interaction = one tap or one scroll gesture. Room navigation = 2 per room on phone, 1 on iPad two-pane.
  • Ratings barely move (41 to 39). Savings come from photos, irrelevant fields, finding work and submit.

Offline and weak signal

Once a property is downloaded, a full inspection runs with no signal. That removes an est. 12–17 min of re-keying per inspection on weak-signal sites.

Per inspection, weak-signal site Before: paper fallback After
Walk the unit Paper checklist; photos in the phone camera In the app; every tap saved on the device
Re-key 41 items est. 7–10 min (10–15 s per item) 0
Match photos to items est. 5–7 min 0
Submit Wait for "full bars" Queued to submit; sends on reconnect
Progress lost on a dropped connection All of it None

Rules the design holds to

  • Download a property from Uploads while connected; it then works with no signal.
  • Submitting offline runs the completeness check on the device and marks the inspection Queued to submit.
  • The due clock stops when the inspector finishes on the device. Weak signal never makes anyone late.
  • Photos upload one at a time, oldest first. On weak signal a small preview goes first; full size follows on Wi-Fi.
  • A failed photo stays on the device and on its row until it uploads or the inspector deletes it.
  • Logout warns and names every unsynced inspection. Primary action: Stay and Sync.
  • v1 assumes one editor per inspection; concurrent edits are not merged.

Assumptions, risks and next steps

Every number marked est. comes from the design model, not field data. Treat them as targets until the field test reports.

Risks

  • Offline needs the template, unit and tenant data on the device first. If staff skip the download, the app has nothing to work from.
  • The "already submitted" defect may share a root cause with retried saves. Engineering needs one idempotent submit path.
  • Auto-calculated interest depends on where deposit data lives (PV CRM or an external PMS).
  • Prototype interest rates are samples; confirm against the RTB before any customer sees a figure.
  • Rating buttons, filter chips and the type filter still sit under 48pt in early boards.

Next steps

  • ☐ Pull the started → submitted baseline per client
  • ☐ Instrument opened → finished timestamps per inspection type
  • ☐ Run the airplane-mode iPad field test at an FSR site

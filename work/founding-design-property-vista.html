Why now

The case I made to leadership

In my first weeks I wrote two short memos for our leadership: one on how design should operate in an AI-first product company, one on how design and engineering should work together. Both started from the same observation.

What changed

  • Product complexity is increasing
  • AI and rapid prototyping are compressing feedback cycles
  • Speed and quality are no longer trade-offs

The challenge

Traditional design processes, built for linear phases and gated approvals, can't keep up. They reduce risk, but they also reduce learning.

AI changes the cost structure of design. Prototyping is now cheaper than documentation.

The old model
  • Standardized processes and frameworks
  • Linear phases and gated approvals
  • Artifacts as the main way to align
  • Rigid, sequential, slow
The model I set up
  • Research, prototyping and testing run continuously
  • Teams move by risk and uncertainty, not by phase
  • Learning decides the next action
  • Flexibility adds speed without losing quality
Operating model

Judgment over process

Design is a decision-making function. Its core value is experienced judgment: pattern recognition from shipped products, market exposure and real user behaviour. So instead of enforcing steps, I set principles.

Start where uncertainty is highest
Point design at the riskiest unknown first, not the first screen in the flow.
Do the smallest thing that reduces risk
A prototype, a transcript review, a five-user test. Whatever answers the question fastest.
Let evidence pick the next step
Learning decides what comes next, not a template.
Skip steps that don't add learning
Consistency in thinking, not in sequence.
What we kept from process
  • A shared language
  • Alignment checkpoints
  • Governance where risk is high
What we removed
  • Performative artifacts
  • One-size-fits-all flows
  • Process as a proxy for quality

Less emphasis on

  • Number of deliverables
  • Strict adherence to frameworks
  • Perfect upfront clarity

More emphasis on

  • Speed to insight
  • Decision quality
  • Measurable product impact

Process scales teams. Experienced judgment scales impact.

Engineering

Design as an accelerator, not a gate

"Good UX reduces entropy. Bad UX creates it."

UX exists to clarify decisions, not slow them. If design adds friction for engineers, it's failing. Lower cognitive load for users and developers means fewer mid-sprint changes, fewer undefined states and fewer post-release fixes.

One system, not two teams
Align early on constraints and trade-offs, and design within technical reality. I'd rather adjust a design early than defend it late.
Ship in layers
The MVP is the core decision flow, not a stripped UI. Reuse patterns aggressively and iterate on real usage, not opinions.
Prevent downstream cost
Clearer intent, defined states and edge cases, and shared understanding before build. UX is cheaper than refactoring.
Pristine handoff
Specs with every state and edge case, tokens mapped to code, and handoff straight into Claude Code and Figma Dev Mode.

For AI-driven features, trust is part of the spec

  • Make the "why" visible, not just the "what": sources, confidence and recency
  • Separate facts, inferences and predictions clearly
  • Human in the loop by default

Opaque AI creates UX debt, and engineering debt.

Platform

Layout, discoverability and feature adoption

As the platform grew from one agent to an ecosystem, the risk shifted from "can it do this?" to "can anyone find it?". I own the information architecture and platform layout, and treat feature adoption as a design outcome, not a marketing one.

The adoption loop we design for

DiscoverFeatures surface in context, at the moment they help.
TrySafe defaults and previews before anything goes live.
AdoptProgress and outcomes shown back to the team.
ExpandProven value earns the next feature its place.
Signal

Loops that keep design honest

A design function without signal is just taste. I set up feedback that runs continuously, from structured advisory boards to AI-simulated users, and closes the loop with prototypes customers can touch.

01Customer advisory boardOperators who shape the roadmap, on a regular cadence
02In-product signalFeedback, transcripts and adoption data
03Synthetic testingBreadth: AI-simulated users across scenarios
04Solo sessionsDepth: one-on-one with real operators and renters
05Triage with ProductShared priorities with the VP Product and PMM
06Ship and showPrototypes and motion demos back to customers

Synthetic plus solo testing

Synthetic users give breadth: hundreds of simulated sessions across personas and edge cases in hours. Solo sessions give depth: watching one real operator or renter is still where the best insight comes from.

Prototypes and motion for customers

AI-powered prototypes and motion demos let customers react to the future before it's built, in sales conversations and advisory board sessions alike.

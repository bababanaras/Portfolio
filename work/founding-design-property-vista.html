The mandate

Two north stars

  • Frictionless workflow. Every task a renter, resident or property team does should take fewer steps than it did yesterday, and none should need a manual.
  • Time to value. From signed contract to visible ROI, as short as possible. If a buyer can't see the value, it doesn't exist yet.

In practice that meant owning the whole surface, from a renter's first message to the economic buyer's ROI report:

The case I made

In my first weeks I wrote two short memos for leadership: how design should operate in an AI-first company, and how design and engineering should work together. Both started from the same observation: AI changes the cost structure of design. Prototyping is now cheaper than documentation.

The old modelThe model I set up
Linear phases and gated approvalsResearch, prototyping and testing run continuously
Artifacts as the way to alignTeams move by risk and uncertainty, not by phase
Process as a proxy for qualityLearning decides the next action

Process scales teams. Experienced judgment scales impact.

Operating model

Judgment over process

  • Start where uncertainty is highest. Point design at the riskiest unknown first, not the first screen in the flow.
  • Do the smallest thing that reduces risk. A prototype, a transcript review, a five-user test.
  • Let evidence pick the next step. Learning decides what comes next, not a template.
  • Skip steps that don't add learning. Consistency in thinking, not in sequence.

Engineering

Design as an accelerator, not a gate

If design adds friction for engineers, it is failing. I align early on constraints, ship in layers (the MVP is the core decision flow, not a stripped UI), and hand off specs with every state and edge case, tokens mapped to code, straight into Claude Code and Figma Dev Mode.

For AI features, trust is part of the spec: make the why visible (sources, confidence, recency), separate facts from inferences and predictions, and keep a human in the loop by default.

The stack (Claude, Claude Code, Codex, Figma Make) cut concept-to-prototype time from weeks to days. This portfolio was designed and built the same way.

Value and platform

Making value visible to the person who signs

AI products live or die on whether the buyer can see the return. I own the ROI story in the sales demo, the executive dashboards after launch, and the value-realization curve: connected on day 1, first AI-led tours and leases in week 1, ROI in view by month 1, and portfolio-wide expansion by quarter 1. These views were used in sales and funding conversations that supported 2 enterprise deals.

Signal

A design function without signal is just taste. Synthetic users give breadth: hundreds of simulated sessions across personas and edge cases in hours. Solo sessions with real operators and renters give depth. A customer advisory board shapes the roadmap, and prototypes and motion demos go back to customers before anything is built.

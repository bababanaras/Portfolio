Fairstone Bank · 2025 · Enterprise B2B

Deposit Broker Portal: eight weeks of follow-ups to ten minutes

We lost a key investment client, and brokers told us why: slow, opaque follow-ups. I led the UX strategy and design of a net-new portal that made servicing transparent and self-serve.

Role
Senior Manager, Product Design: strategy, architecture and design leadership
Product
Broker portal for GIC and deposit products
Market
Canadian deposit brokerage
Scope
Release one · 170+ screens · SAP, DASH and Dynamics
8 wks → 10 minApplication creation and processing turnaround
170+Screens in release one, on a new design system
0Email chains needed to track an application

The problem

The product wasn't broken. The servicing was.

Oaken GICs and savings were competitive, and much of the money arrived through brokers. Servicing them ran on email, phone calls, spreadsheets and manual updates across SAP, DASH and Dynamics. Brokers could not see where an application stood, so they chased internal teams, and internal teams acted as human middleware.

That was a revenue risk, not a usability inconvenience: brokers move capital to whoever services faster and more transparently.

Discovery

We mapped workflows end to end and interviewed brokers, servicing teams and operations. The same data was entered again and again, statuses conflicted between systems, and documents moved by hand.

Strategy

Transparent, system-orchestrated, low-touch

  • A greenfield portal. 170+ screens on our new enterprise design system: a platform build, not a feature release.
  • Workflow orchestration. Status definitions, triggers, data ownership and exception handling aligned across SAP, DASH and Dynamics. Tribal knowledge became structured flows.
  • Principles. Transparency by default. Self-serve over follow-up. Automation before human escalation. Exception-based servicing.

"When there's a delay, I want to understand why immediately, so I don't need to chase internal teams."

The work

Confidence and control over decoration

Prominent lifecycle indicators and stage labels, critical updates first, timestamped confirmations and audit-friendly interactions.

Validation

  • Workflow validation with operations and edge-case scenario testing
  • Usability walkthroughs with broker representatives
  • Status states, triggers and error states documented for engineering, with components aligned to the design system

My role

I framed the problem as a trust and revenue risk, secured cross-functional buy-in, drove the enterprise workflow mapping and owned UX architecture across 170+ screens.

What I learned

  • In broker distribution, servicing experience directly affects revenue.
  • Transparency reduces operational burden.
  • UX leadership in financial services has to think in systems, not features.

Outcome

Application turnaround went from eight weeks to ten minutes. Brokers track every application without a single email chain, internal teams engage only on exceptions, and the gap that cost us a key client is closed.

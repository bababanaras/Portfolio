Thinking

Design is a decision-making discipline. My job is to make the right decisions visible, and the wrong ones impossible to ship.

Two halves of the same practice: how I design AI agents people can trust, and how I lead design and build teams so the right calls get made fast.

Part one · Designing agents

Designing AI-native products users can trust.

01

Autonomy is a dial, not a switch

Every action an agent can take gets its own setting: decide, ask, or hand off. Two questions set it. How much does a mistake cost? Can it be undone? Not: what can the model technically do?

  • Booking a tourDecides
  • Changing a move-in dateAsks
  • Lease exceptionHands off
02

Design the handoff before the happy path

Trust is won or lost the moment an agent says “this isn’t mine to decide.” I design that moment first: what the renter is told, what the person on the other end receives in Vida, and how fast the loop closes.

03

If it can’t be undone, explain it first

Reversible steps move fast. Irreversible ones slow down, show their reasoning and wait for a yes. Signatures, payments and anything legal never happen silently.

04

Operators need a cockpit, not a chat log

Nobody supervises a portfolio by reading transcripts. Vida leads with what needs a human now, why, and the one action that fixes it.

05

Every AI state is a design state

Thinking, confident, uncertain, escalated, completed. Each one looks and reads the same everywhere, so renters and operators always know where things stand.

Part two · Leading design

Process scales teams. Judgment scales impact.

I keep the artefacts that sharpen thinking and drop the ones that only look productive. The aim is confident decisions and fast learning loops.

The loop

  • Align. Success metrics, owners, timeline01
  • Discover. Research until the problem is sharp02
  • Define. Strategy, architecture, constraints03
  • Design. Iterate and validate with users04
  • Deliver. Handoff, build support, UX QA05
  • Evolve. Measure, learn, feed the roadmap06

How I partner

  • With product. PMs co-own the experience. I help shape the roadmap by user impact, and push back on scope with data, not ego.
  • With engineering. Engineers are design partners. I bring them in at discovery and check feasibility before committing to a pattern.
  • Handoff is a ceremony. Annotated specs with states, edge cases and accessibility notes, a live walkthrough, and me on hand through the build.
  • QA is design’s job too. Nothing ships without UX QA against the spec and WCAG 2.1 AA. I sign off builds with the rigour of a code review.
  • A design roadmap. Research sprints, system work and UX debt get their own milestones, so leadership sees design capacity before the crunch.
  • Guardrails built in. Compliance, privacy and accessibility are designed in from the start, not bolted on at legal review.

Always

  • Users first, always.
  • Data informs. Intuition leads.
  • Design is a team sport.
  • Constraints breed creativity.
  • Ship with intention. Iterate with purpose.
  • Accessibility is not optional.

Part three · How I build teams

Teams scale judgment. Rituals don’t.

I’ve led a team of 5 designers at Fairstone Bank, owning hiring, budgeting, scoping and the design backlog, and a multi-country team at WebBeds across Spain, the UK, Romania and Dubai, where we took UX maturity from stage 0 to stage 3. Now I’m building Property Vista’s design function from the ground up. The same principles hold at every size:

  • Hire for judgment and range. I look for designers who can make a good call with half the information, and who can move between research, systems and pixels. A paid work sample beats a whiteboard puzzle.
  • Small, senior, close to the work. Player-coaches over layers. I set direction, review the work in detail, and still design, so the bar is visible rather than described.
  • Clear decision rights. Everyone knows which calls are theirs, which need a review, and which come to me. Fewer meetings, faster and better decisions.
  • Critique as evidence. Crits run on data, user sessions and principles, not opinion or seniority. Disagreements end in an experiment, not a vote.
  • Grow people on two paths. Separate IC and manager tracks, written expectations for each level, and a growth conversation every quarter, not just at review time.
  • Design beyond the design team. At Fairstone I trained 8+ BAs, BSAs and product owners in Figma and design thinking, so good decisions happened without a designer in every room.

The measure is simple: the team makes good calls when I’m not in the room.

TL;DR

I don’t just design screens. I design the conditions for good decisions, for the people on the team and for the agents we ship.
